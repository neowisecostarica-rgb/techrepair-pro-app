# TRP / MB3 — Naming Sweep: Japanese Discipline / Warrior / Mastery Line

Date: 2026-09-16
Status: exploratory naming research, NOT legal trademark clearance

## Objective
Find a commercial name with the emotional territory Gustavo identified through MAXIMUS → VICIUS → RONIN → SAMURAI: short, strong, premium, disciplined, precise, international, and not literally descriptive of repair software.

## Search principle
Prefer authentic Japanese concepts where possible. Reject or downgrade names already materially occupied in SaaS/software/operations, even if the meaning is excellent. A web collision scan is only preliminary; trademark counsel/database clearance by jurisdiction and Nice classes remains required before launch.

## Names screened / downgraded

### MAXIMUS — HOLD / material conflict
Previously audited. Significant software/technology and trademark saturation.

### RONIN — DOWNGRADE
Excellent concept; materially saturated in technology/software.

### SAMURAI — DOWNGRADE
Powerful archetype but extremely generic/saturated internationally.

### TAKUMI — REJECT for current purpose
Meaning is excellent: artisan/master/craftsman. However, TAKUMI has active software-related trademark history and multiple technology/software businesses. Too occupied for the clean international brand objective.

### KODO — REJECT
Short and strong, but Kodo is already an active technology/software name and has SaaS trademark activity, including class 42 contexts.

### KENSEI — REJECT
Strong 'sword fighter/sword saint' territory, but software/computer-game registrations and technology use create avoidable collision.

### KAIZEN — REJECT
Globally understood operational-improvement concept but highly generic/saturated in consulting and software; active operations platforms use it.

### SEIDO — REJECT
Excellent phonetics and precision/order associations, but there are already active SaaS/business and operations products called Seido.

### SHINRAI — REJECT
Means trust/reliability, but active technology and operations SaaS companies already use Shinrai.

### KIZUNA — REJECT
Means bond/connection. Multiple active business-management, SaaS, AI and technology products already use Kizuna.

### KOKORO — REJECT
Strong Japanese concept but active operations-management software already exists under Kokoro.

### KIWAMI — REJECT
Means extreme/ultimate/mastery territory. An active all-in-one operations/CRM platform already uses KIWAMI with 'One Platform. Total Control' positioning—too close to TRP's intended territory.

### SHUGYO — REJECT HARD
Meaning is nearly perfect: disciplined practice in pursuit of mastery. But an active company already positions Shugyo as an 'Executive Operating System' for operational visibility. This is directly adjacent to our own OS/operations positioning.

### ZANSHIN — REJECT HARD
Powerful martial concept of continued awareness/readiness, but active AI/business software and a registered/claimed cybersecurity SaaS solution already use ZANSHIN.

### SHUGO — REJECT
Already used by a SaaS workforce-management business acquired by SwipeClock.

### YOROI — REJECT
Means armor. Strong metaphor, but highly recognized as a software/crypto wallet and software ecosystem name.

### MONONOFU — DOWNGRADE
Old Japanese term associated with warrior/samurai. Distinctive internationally but Japanese trademark activity includes class 9 and gaming/software-adjacent registrations. Also longer and less intuitive in Spanish/English.

### KAGEMUSHA — DOWNGRADE
Historically 'shadow warrior/double'. Visually strong but long, has software/game trademark history and current developer-tool usage. Meaning also points toward a double/stand-in rather than operational mastery.

### TATSUJIN — DOWNGRADE
Means expert/master. Strong semantic fit, but Bandai Namco's TAIKO NO TATSUJIN has substantial software/game trademark footprint and TATSUJIN itself has international trademark activity. Avoid for a clean global path.

## Surviving conceptual directions for deeper clearance

### BUJIN
Japanese martial term commonly rendered as warrior/martial person. Strong, compact, easy in Spanish and English, and visually premium. Initial broad web scan did not surface a directly competing operations SaaS named BUJIN in the same way as TAKUMI, SHUGYO, KIWAMI, SEIDO or KIZUNA. This is NOT clearance; it earns a deeper trademark/domain check.

Brand territory: discipline, mastery, readiness, strength without the generic SAMURAI label.
Potential concern: martial framing can become aggressive if the visual identity leans into weapons/costume. Direction should be abstract, precise and executive.

### BUSHIN
Japanese martial-spirit territory. Initial broad scan surfaced martial-arts marks and unrelated uses but did not surface a direct operations SaaS collision in this pass. Requires exact class 9/42 and international clearance.

Brand territory: spirit + discipline + mastery.
Potential concern: 'bushin' has multiple cultural/martial uses and may be less ownable than a coined mark.

### ISAMU
Japanese given name associated with courage/bravery. Clean phonetics, human and premium. Search did not surface an obvious operations SaaS collision in this pass, but ISAMU NOGUCHI is a famous protected name/mark in design/furniture contexts, so broader clearance and brand-confusion analysis are mandatory.

Brand territory: courage, capability, quiet confidence.
Potential concern: reads as a person's name rather than a system unless identity is exceptionally strong.

## Strategic conclusion
The search exposed a useful pattern: the most beautiful authentic Japanese operational concepts are often already occupied in software. Therefore the best next naming territory is likely **Japanese-inspired but coined**, rather than taking a famous dictionary word such as Samurai, Kaizen, Takumi, Kizuna or Ronin.

A coined mark can preserve the sound system and values—discipline, precision, mastery, control—while improving distinctiveness, domain options, SEO and trademark defensibility.

## Next naming pass
Create coined candidates using Japanese phonetic inspiration without falsely assigning Japanese meanings. Requirements:
- 4–7 letters preferred;
- 2–3 syllables;
- pronounceable ES/EN;
- strong wordmark silhouette;
- no repair/tech cliché;
- no accidental negative meaning in ES/EN;
- preliminary exact-match software/SaaS search before presenting finalists;
- deeper trademark/domain review only after 3–5 finalists survive.
